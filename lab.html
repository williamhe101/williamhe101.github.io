The Lab 🧪

Welcome to the lab. Six things to poke at, watch or hover over. They're simplified models and toys, not real design data, but the ideas behind them are very real.

1 · Reflections on a PCB trace (3D)

Meet a bit on a very short commute. Add a via stub, a dead-end piece of trace, and the bit splits up: an inverted reflection runs back toward the source, while a delayed copy comes back out of the stub's open end. Everything bounces off the source again and shows up at the receiver as echoes, the ghosts that keep signal integrity engineers employed. Drag to look around.

2 · Equalization opens a closed eye

An eye diagram is basically a link's report card, and right now this one is failing. A lossy channel smears bits into each other until the eye slams shut. Switch on CTLE, FFE and DFE, the same tools I use at work, and watch the rainbow untangle.

3 · Crosstalk and spacing

Traces make terrible neighbors: every time the loud one switches, the quiet one hears about it. The top view shows the noise, and the cross-section shows why. Drag the slider to pull them apart and watch the field rings fade, or flip the checkbox to hide the full EM field if it gets too busy.

4 · DormPod: my senior design project (3D)

My senior design project, as a short film. The pod pulls up to the curb, rolls into the dorm and scans the hallway with lidar. It reads AprilTags on the wall to find the right door, pops its lid and drops the package in Room B. No remote control, no hands: just sit back and watch it do its job.

5 · Magic Mirror

My smart mirror, set up the way most people do. Hit the power button: a compliment fades out of the glass first (it's the mirror's job to be nice to you), then the clock, weather and a rotating set of everyday modules. Everything shown is demo data.

6 · E-ink Pokémon card display (3D)

Old Kindle and Nook e-readers deserve better than a drawer, so I'm turning them into e-ink Pokémon card displays. This one is a 3D Kindle 4, the 2011 model. Hover left to right to flip through cards, then be patient: real e-ink flashes, develops the new card in steps and leaves a faint ghost of the last one.
